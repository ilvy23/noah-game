> You pull into a street that is not on any map you have ever used.

The house is brick, like everything else, but older. The bricks are laid
in a pattern you cannot name — not herringbone, not bond, something
between. The front door is dark wood, waxed by someone who is still
waxing it.

You inherited this. That is what the paper says. You cannot remember the
name on the paper. You cannot remember the drive. You remember the keys
in your hand and the smell of the forest at the edge of town and a voice
in the back of your head saying *correct, correct, correct*.

The valley is behind the house. You can see it from the kitchen window:
a long, low fold of land that the town does not talk about. The lake is in
it. The forest is in it. The old castle is in it. The valley does not
sleep. The valley waits. You know this the way you know your own name,
though you do not know how you know it.

On the hallway table, in a seal of wax the color of a bruise that healed
wrong, is a letter. It is addressed to whoever comes next.

It is addressed to you.

~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
LÜNEN — THE DARK TOWN
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

The town of Lünen is a long, low town that sits in the bottom
of a valley it does not mention. The houses are brick, the
streets are narrow, and the streetlights come on at four
o’clock in the afternoon, every day, whether the sun is out or not.

You know the saying before you’ve heard it. It is on the wall
of the bakery, in the window of the pharmacy, painted in white
on the side of the old water tower: "Das Tal schläft nicht.
Es wartet." The valley doesn’t sleep. It waits.

The people here are not unkind. They are *correct*. They say
good morning with the exact right amount of voice. They do not
ask where you are from. They ask when you arrived, and they
seem to already know the answer, and they seem a little
disappointed that it matches.

The town is quiet. The streetlights are on. The bakery has
bread. The butcher has bread. The pharmacy has bread. You
are not sure which shop you walked into. You are not sure
you walked into a shop.
